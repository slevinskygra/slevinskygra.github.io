30 Sep 2026

Active nanochannels that break symmetry, out in Physical Review Letters

Our paper with Ahis Shrestha and Monica Olvera de la Cruz shows that a nanochannel with perfectly symmetric walls can start pumping fluid on its own. When the walls combine surface-charge patches with active ionic fluxes, the electrolyte breaks its left–right symmetry above a critical flux and develops a directed flow, without any applied voltage or pressure. The interactive sketch on the home page shows the idea.

Read the paper in Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 148202 (2026).

I took part in the CECAM–WE Heraeus school Nonequilibrium physics in nanoconfinement at the École de Physique des Houches, in the French Alps, where I presented a poster on how active ionic fluxes induce symmetry breaking in charge-patterned nanochannels.

7 Jul 2026

Charge clouds and rectification in patchy nanochannels, in Faraday Discussions

With Gabriele Dalla Valle, Tine Curk and Ignacio Pagonabarraga, we show that charged patches on the walls of a straight nanochannel trap clouds of counterions, and that these clouds give diode-like rectification of the ionic current when a field and a pressure gradient act together, with no geometric asymmetry at all.

Read the open-access paper in Faraday Discussions.

23 Jan 2026

Self-oscillating synchronematic colloids, in Nature Communications

Our study with Zhengyan Zhang, Monica Olvera de la Cruz and Kyle Bishop shows that Quincke colloids rocking back and forth under a steady electric field synchronize through the fluid around them and align their orientations, a collective state we call synchronematic order. Northwestern covered the work in the press release “A hidden rhythm brings microscopic particles into unison”.

Read the open-access paper in Nat. Commun. 17, 1841 (2026).

Our letter with Tine Curk and Ignacio Pagonabarraga on the discontinuous transition between slow, ionic and fast, Poiseuille flow in charge-patterned nanochannels was selected as an Editors’ Suggestion in Physical Review Letters and covered in the Physics synopsis “Gated flows in charged channels”.

Read the paper in Phys. Rev. Lett. 133, 078201 (2024).
