Fluid-mediated interactions and self-organized transport, from nanochannels to active colloids

Sergi G. Leyva1,2

1Center for Computation and Theory of Soft Materials, Northwestern University, Evanston, IL, USA
2Department of Physics and Astronomy, Northwestern University, Evanston, IL, USA

I am a theoretical and computational physicist working on soft matter far from equilibrium. I study how hydrodynamic and electrostatic couplings make ions, colloids and liquids organize themselves under confinement: nanochannels that pump fluid with no applied voltage, self-oscillating colloids that synchronize through the liquid around them, and liquids that invade channels faster when the walls are lubricated.

My work combines lattice-Boltzmann, dissipative particle dynamics and Stokesian dynamics simulations with analytical theory, often side by side with experiments. I am a postdoctoral researcher with Monica Olvera de la Cruz at Northwestern University, after a PhD in statistical physics at the University of Barcelona.

Research

All research projects

Nanofluidics and iontronics

How surface-charge patterns and active ionic fluxes gate, rectify and even spontaneously drive transport through nanochannels, the building blocks of iontronic and neuromorphic devices.

Active and self-oscillating colloids

Self-oscillating Quincke colloids that synchronize and align through the surrounding fluid, and catalytic colloidal rafts that switch propulsion mechanism as they grow.

Driven colloids and capillary flows

Magnetically driven colloids that synchronize, cluster and clog in structured landscapes, and liquids that imbibe faster through channels coated with a lubricant.
